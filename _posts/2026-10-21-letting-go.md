title: "The Trouble with Letting Go"
date: 2026-10-21
---

One of the hardest things about losing someone is that your mind does not always accept what has happened.

A relationship ends, but your mind keeps working on it. You go through old conversations, think about things you could have said differently, and try to understand what went wrong. You imagine different versions of the past, as if finding the right one could change the present.

And sometimes, the more you think about it, the worse you feel.

I think there is something strange about the way we deal with problems when we are emotionally attached to them. We can spend hours looking for complicated solutions to something that might have a very simple answer. We try to fix things, explain things, and find another way. We keep looking for a door even when the room has no exit.

Maybe we do this because accepting the simple answer hurts too much.

If a relationship is over, one possible answer is that it is over. It does not mean that everything was a lie, that the good moments were meaningless, or that the other person was a bad person. It only means that something that once worked no longer works, or that two people can no longer build the same life together.

But this answer is often not enough for us. We want a better explanation. We want to know exactly why it happened. We want to find the one mistake that, if we could go back and change it, would make everything right again.

We treat the past like a problem that has not been solved yet.

And maybe that is where some of the pain comes from. We are not only sad about losing someone. We are also fighting the fact that we cannot change what happened.

There is another thing I find difficult to accept: sometimes, we are willing to put up with a lot just to avoid being alone.

We are afraid of losing someone, so we accept things we would never advise a friend to accept. We make excuses. We lower our expectations. We tell ourselves that things will get better, that everyone has problems, or that maybe we are asking for too much.

Of course, no relationship is perfect. People make mistakes, misunderstand each other, and hurt each other without always meaning to. Love requires patience, and it would be unreasonable to expect everything to be easy.

But there must be a difference between being patient with another person and slowly giving up on yourself to keep them around.

The fact that losing someone hurts does not mean that keeping them is the right choice.

I think we sometimes forget that a relationship is a choice between two people, not a sentence that we have to serve until things get better. We should be able to leave when staying becomes wrong for us. We do not need to prove that things were terrible enough, or that the other person was bad enough, before we are allowed to walk away.

And we do not have to hate someone to accept that we cannot be together anymore.

This is probably one of the hardest parts for me. It is easier to leave when you are angry. It is easier when you can point to something terrible and tell yourself that the decision is obvious. But what do you do when you still care about someone? What do you do when there are good memories, when you miss them, and when a part of you still wants things to work?

I don't know if there is a simple answer to that.

Maybe you just have to accept that two things can be true at the same time: you can miss someone and still know that the relationship is over. You can love what you had and still believe that it should not continue. You can wish things had gone differently without spending the rest of your life trying to change them.

I am not saying that this makes moving on easy. Knowing something and accepting it are two different things. Sometimes, your mind understands a situation long before your feelings catch up.

And I think that is okay.

There is no need to turn every painful experience into a lesson immediately. There is no need to pretend that you are grateful for what happened, or that everything was supposed to happen this way. Sometimes something ends, and it hurts. You can be sad about it without needing to find a greater meaning behind it.

But I don't want to make my own life harder by treating every ending as a problem that must somehow be reversed.

I don't want to spend all my time searching for another explanation, another possibility, or another version of the past in which things worked out differently. I don't want to turn a painful situation into an even more painful one just because I cannot accept the simplest answer.

Maybe the answer is not to stop caring. Maybe it is to stop asking the past to become something else.

I still have to learn how to do that.

There is a passage from Alfred, Lord Tennyson's *Ulysses* that I keep coming back to:

> I am a part of all that I have met;
> Yet all experience is an arch wherethro'
> Gleams that untravell'd world whose margin fades
> For ever and forever when I move.
>
> How dull it is to pause, to make an end,
> To rust unburnish'd, not to shine in use!
> As tho' to breathe were life! Life piled on life
> Were all too little, and of one to me
> Little remains: but every hour is saved
> From that eternal silence, something more,
> A bringer of new things; and vile it were
> For some three suns to store and hoard myself,
> And this gray spirit yearning in desire
> To follow knowledge like a sinking star,
> Beyond the utmost bound of human thought.

Maybe moving on is not about leaving the past behind completely. Maybe it is about understanding that the past has become part of us, while there is still a world ahead that we have not seen.

For now, I am trying to understand that not everything can be fixed, not every question has an answer that will make me feel better, and not everyone who leaves my life has to become a villain for me to let them go.

I am trying to understand that not everything can be fixed, not every question has an answer that will make me feel better, and not everyone who leaves my life has to become a villain for me to let them go.

Sometimes, a relationship ends. Sometimes, you don't get the explanation you wanted. And sometimes, the only thing left to do is to let something be over, even when a part of you is not ready.

I think that is what I am trying to learn: how to let something end without letting it destroy everything that comes after it.
